Sheet B of E Mark, color and type
B1 · The markCurrent REWAP logo, unchanged

The logo stays exactly as it is.

The current REWAP mark is the primary identity: a navy key carrying the REWAP wordmark in gold and white, with a skyline along its shaft. The Atlas system is designed around it rather than replacing it.

Min. size
Horizontal logo 150 px wide on screen, 1.5 in in print. Compact mark 32 px.
Grounds
Paper, white or Frame tint. On navy or photography, place it on a paper Title Block.

Two production notes for the client

The only public logo files are a 380 px PNG and a 300 px PNG. Vector masters (SVG or EPS) are needed before launch, so the logo stays sharp on high-density screens and in print.

The mark already contains a key, a roofline and a skyline. Because the logo says “real estate,” the rest of the system never repeats those motifs. It speaks about place instead: parcels, streets, materials and terrain.

Misuse

Directly on navy. The key disappears into the ground.

Stretched or condensed to fit a space.

Recolored, including “seasonal” or material colors.

Rotated, shadowed or given effects.

B2 · ColorLogo core + material palette

Every color names a material.

Navy and Gold come straight from the logo. The supporting palette borrows its logic from fire-insurance atlases, where color recorded what a building was made of. Here Brick, Frame and Stone describe the actual fabric of Massachusetts: mill brick from Lowell to Fall River, clapboard three-deckers and capes, granite and slate.

Proportion of use across a typical page: Paper carries reading, Navy owns whole regions (navigation, the map, footers), materials fill the map and data, Gold is reserved for wayfinding: routes, the selected place and the primary action.

B3 · TypographyCivic serif, wayfinding grotesk, survey numerals

Three voices: the editor, the signmaker, the surveyor.

A sturdy early-twentieth-century serif for authority, a grotesk with a true width axis for wayfinding and interface, and a mono reserved for measured things: coordinates, parcel and MLS numbers.

Brygada 1918

From the Berkshires to the Cape: 351 cities and towns, each with its own streets.

Revived from a Polish typeface of 1918. It reads as civic and confident without imitating anything antique. Used for display, headlines, ledes and long-form Field Notes.
